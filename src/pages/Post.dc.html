Sliding glass door leaking water when it rains? Causes & fixes.

Puddle by the patio door after every storm? You're not alone. It's one of the most common calls we get in Florida — and most leaks are cheap and easy to fix.

Gators Sliding Doors teamWritten by the techs who fix these doors · 5.0 on Google
Quick test: find the leak Close and lock the door. Have someone inside watch while you spray a garden hose (gentle flow) on the bottom track first, then the middle where the panels meet, then the sides and top. Where the water shows up tells you the cause.

5 common causes (and fixes)

01

Clogged weep holes

#1 cause

Small drain holes on the outside of the track let rainwater escape. When dirt and sand block them, the track fills up and overflows inside.

Fix: Vacuum the track and clear each hole with a pipe cleaner or zip tie. Pour a cup of water in the track to make sure it drains. Full steps: how to clean sliding door tracks.

Never caulk the weep holes. It makes the leak worse.
02

Worn weatherstripping

Florida sun flattens the seals along the door edges. Rain then blows right through.

Fix: Replace the worn strips. Keep rubber seals soft with silicone spray (not WD-40).

03

Worn rollers (door is sagging)

Bad rollers make the door drop and leave a gap at the top. If your door also drags or won't latch, this is likely it.

Fix: Try the adjustment screws at the bottom corners. If that doesn't work, you need new rollers or a pro roller repair.

04

Damaged track

Dents, cracks or salt-air corrosion stop the door from sealing.

Fix: A worn track can often be covered with a screw-down track cover. Cracked tracks need track repair.

05

Cracked caulk around the frame

Old caulk on the outside of the frame lets water in along the sides.

Fix: Remove the old caulk and reseal the outside edge of the frame with exterior silicone.

Only leaks in big storms?

Strong wind can push rain over the sill even on a healthy door. Keep weep holes clear, lock the door so it seals tight, and see our hurricane prep guide.

When to call a pro

  • It still leaks after cleaning the track
  • The door sags, drags or won't lock
  • You see soft floors, swollen baseboards or mold
  • It's an impact door

FAQ

Is water in my sliding door track normal?

+

A little during rain, yes. It should drain within minutes. If it sits for hours, the weep holes are clogged.

Should I caulk the track to stop the leak?

+

No. Only caulk the outside of the frame where it meets the wall. Sealing the track traps water.

Do I need a new door?

+

Usually not. Cleaning, new seals, new rollers or a track repair fix most leaks.
